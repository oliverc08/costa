## If your Medi-Cal ended because of missing paperwork (90-day cure period)

If your Medi-Cal ended because you did not return your renewal form or the information the county asked for, you have 90 days from the date your coverage ended to fix it. This is called the 90-day cure period.

- Turn in the signed renewal form and all of the missing information to your county within those 90 days.
- You do not need to fill out a new application.
- If the county finds you still qualify, your Medi-Cal is restored back to the date it ended, with no gap in coverage.
- You must send everything that was requested. If you only send part of it, your Medi-Cal may stay closed until the county gets the rest.

If it has been more than 90 days, you must submit a new Medi-Cal application.

## Three-month grace period for some adults

Some adults affected by the 2026 enrollment freeze have a three-month grace period after their full-scope Medi-Cal ends to sign up again. After that, they can only get restricted-scope Medi-Cal. See "Medi-Cal changes in 2026 and 2027."

## Other coverage after Medi-Cal ends

If you lose Medi-Cal, you may be able to enroll in a Covered California health plan. You have up to 90 days after Medi-Cal ends to request Covered California coverage through a special enrollment period. Call Covered California at 1-800-300-1506.

## If you disagree with a decision: State Hearing

If you think the county's decision is wrong, you can ask for a State Hearing. You have 90 days from the date on the Notice of Action to ask. After 90 days, you must show a good reason for asking late.

Ways to ask for a State Hearing:
- Fill out the "Request for State Hearing" on the back of your Notice of Action and send it to the county address on the notice.
- Online at the California Department of Social Services hearing request page.
- By phone: California Department of Social Services, (800) 743-8525 (TDD (800) 952-8349).

When you ask, tell them your language (and dialect) so they can provide an interpreter at the hearing.

If your problem is with your Medi-Cal managed care health plan (not the county), you generally must first file an appeal with your plan within 60 days of the plan's notice.

## Free legal help with Medi-Cal problems

The Health Consumer Alliance gives free help with Medi-Cal problems, including denials and loss of coverage: 1-888-804-3536.
