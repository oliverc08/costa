## How Medi-Cal renewal works

Medi-Cal must check every year that you still qualify. This is called renewal (redetermination).

First, your county tries to renew you automatically using information it already has. If it can, you will get a notice saying you are renewed for one year, and you do not need to do anything else.

If the county cannot renew you automatically, it mails you a renewal form in a bright yellow envelope. The form is pre-filled with information the county has.

## What to do with the yellow renewal form

- Read the pre-filled information. If anything is wrong, correct it on the form.
- Send the proof the form asks for, such as pay stubs or tax returns.
- Sign the form.
- Return it by the due date on the form. You can turn it in online at BenefitsCal.com, by mail, by phone, or in person at your county office.
- If you get a renewal form and do not complete it, your Medi-Cal will end.

You can keep using Medi-Cal while the county reviews your renewal. It only ends if you are no longer eligible, and then you will get a notice that says the date it ends.

## After you turn in your renewal

If something is missing, the county will send you a notice asking for more information. If you still qualify, you will get a notice that your Medi-Cal is renewed for one year. If you do not qualify, the notice tells you when your Medi-Cal ends and how to ask for a State Hearing if you disagree.

## Keep your address and phone number up to date

Renewal forms are sent by mail. If you move or change your phone number, tell your county within 10 days so you do not miss your renewal. You can update your information on BenefitsCal.com or by calling your county office.

## How often you renew

Today, most people renew Medi-Cal once a year. Starting in 2027, some adults ages 19 to 64 will renew every six months; see "Medi-Cal changes in 2026 and 2027."
