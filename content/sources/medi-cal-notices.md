## Common Medi-Cal letters

- Renewal form (often in a bright yellow envelope; for most people it is form MC 216): the county needs you to confirm or update your information and send proof so you can keep Medi-Cal. Return it by the due date.
- Request for information or verification (form MC 355): the county needs specific documents, such as pay stubs, before it can decide. Send exactly what is listed by the due date.
- Notice of Action (NOA) approving Medi-Cal: you are approved. It says when coverage starts.
- Notice of Action denying or ending (discontinuing) Medi-Cal: it says why and the date coverage ends. The back of the notice explains how to ask for a State Hearing.
- Notice of Action changing your benefits: your coverage type or cost is changing.

## What to look for on any letter

- The date the letter was mailed (often labeled "Date" or "Mailing Date").
- The due date: when the county must receive what it asks for.
- What the county is asking you to do or send.
- The phone number of your county worker or office.
- Your rights, including how to ask for a State Hearing.

## Deadlines that matter

- Renewal forms and requests for information: return them by the due date on the letter, or your Medi-Cal can end.
- If Medi-Cal ended because paperwork was missing: you have 90 days after it ended to send everything and have it restored without a new application.
- If you disagree with a decision: you have 90 days from the date of the Notice of Action to ask for a State Hearing. Keep the envelope, because it shows when the notice was mailed.

## Asking for a State Hearing about a notice

Fill out the "Request for State Hearing" on the back of the Notice of Action and send it to the county address on the notice, request one online through the California Department of Social Services, or call (800) 743-8525. Tell them your language and dialect so an interpreter can be arranged.
